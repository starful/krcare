Patients often remember prescription bottles but forget the fish oil in their carry-on, the sleep gummies they take nightly, or the herbal teas marketed as harmless wellness products. In a Korean clinic setting, incomplete medication disclosure is one of the most preventable causes of surgical delays, anesthesia complications, and unexpected bleeding.

Doctors need an honest, complete picture of everything that affects your blood, heart, liver, and immune response. This guide explains what to disclose before cosmetic, dental, vision, or surgical procedures in Korea, how clinics typically handle medication holds, and how to organize information when records are in another language.

## Why Full Disclosure Matters

Anesthesia teams, surgeons, and dermatologists rely on your history to:

* **Avoid drug interactions** with local anesthetics, sedatives, and antibiotics
* **Manage bleeding risk** during and after incisions or dental extractions
* **Adjust plans** for patients on cardiac, psychiatric, or hormone therapies
* **Identify supplements** that prolong healing or interfere with swelling control

Withholding information because you think an item is "natural" or embarrassing can seriously endanger you. Clinics treat disclosures confidentially within the medical team.

## Prescription Medications: Always List These First

Bring a complete list including dose, frequency, and prescribing reason. Common categories requiring special planning include:

* **Blood thinners and anticoagulants** used for clot prevention
* **Antiplatelet medications** often prescribed for heart or stroke history
* **Insulin and oral diabetes medications** requiring perioperative glucose monitoring
* **Blood pressure and heart rhythm drugs** that interact with anesthesia
* **Psychiatric medications** including antidepressants and mood stabilizers
* **Hormone therapies** including contraceptives and gender-affirming hormones
* **Immunosuppressants** for autoimmune conditions or transplants
* **Accutane and related retinoids** with mandatory surgical waiting periods after cessation
* **Recent antibiotic or steroid courses** affecting infection and healing risk

Never stop prescription medications on your own before travel. Ask your home physician and the Korean clinic for coordinated hold or bridge plans.

## Over-the-Counter and Common Supplements

Many international patients forget these, yet clinics scrutinize them closely:

* **Aspirin and NSAIDs** such as ibuprofen and naproxen, which affect bleeding
* **Fish oil, omega-3, and krill oil**
* **Vitamin E** in high doses
* **Garlic, ginkgo biloba, and ginger** supplements marketed for circulation
* **Herbal weight-loss or detox blends** with undisclosed stimulants
* **Sleep aids and antihistamines** that sedate or dry mucous membranes
* **Protein powders and performance supplements** with unclear ingredient lists

When in doubt, photograph every label you take regularly and share images during pre-admission screening.

## Traditional and Herbal Medicines

Korea has a rich tradition of herbal medicine, and many visitors also arrive taking products from their home countries. Traditional does not mean inert. Herbal formulas may contain anticoagulant-like compounds or interact with anesthesia.

Disclose:

* **Korean traditional herbal prescriptions (hanbang)**
* **Chinese patent medicines** in pill or powder form
* **Ayurvedic or Southeast Asian remedies**
* **CBD or cannabis-derived products** where legal in your home jurisdiction; legality in Korea is strict and disclosure still matters medically

Clinic staff are not judging your choices; they are managing safety.

## Recreational Substances and Lifestyle Factors

Be candid about:

* **Alcohol consumption** patterns affecting liver metabolism and anesthesia
* **Nicotine and vaping**, which impair wound healing
* **Recreational drug use** within relevant time windows
* **Recent fasting or extreme diets** affecting electrolytes

Honesty prevents cancellations on the morning of surgery when labs reveal surprises.

## Typical Medication Hold Timelines

Exact stop dates are determined by your physicians, not internet lists. Clinics often follow general principles:

* **Blood-thinning prescriptions** may require supervised holds days to weeks ahead
* **NSAIDs and aspirin** often stop several days before invasive procedures unless cardiologists advise otherwise
* **Fish oil and high-dose vitamins** commonly pause one to two weeks before surgery
* **Retinoids** may require months of cessation before certain laser or surgical plans
* **Herbal products** usually stop at least one to two weeks before anesthesia when possible

Your Korean clinic may request labs or cardiology clearance before confirming dates. Build buffer days into your itinerary for this review.

## How to Prepare Your Medication Document

Create a one- to two-page **Medication and Supplement List** with:

* Generic and brand names
* Dose and time of day taken
* Start date and prescribing doctor
* Last dose taken before travel
* Pharmacy contact in your home country for verification

If records are not in English, use professional translation for critical items or ask your embassy-approved translator. Apps alone are insufficient for this document.

Bring original bottles in carry-on luggage when permitted; customs rules vary. Photos of labels supplement but do not replace clinician review.

## Day-of-Procedure Rules

Follow fasting instructions precisely. Even small sips or chewing gum can delay anesthesia.

* Inform staff of any dose you took accidentally during the hold period
* Report illness, fever, or new medications since your last pre-op call
* Ask when to restart each medication after treatment; restart timing differs for blood thinners and hormones

## Dermatology and Non-Surgical Treatments Still Require Disclosure

Laser sessions, injectables, and chemical peels interact with photosensitizing drugs, recent sun exposure medications, and retinoids. Dental local anesthetic still requires allergy and heart history. Vision surgery screening depends on stable medication effects on dry eye.

Do not assume "non-surgical" means "no questions asked."

## Clinic Verification and Official Listings

Choose providers that conduct structured pre-admission reviews. The **KR Care** interactive map lists clinics from official **Korea Tourism Organization (KTO)** medical-tourism listings, a dependable starting point for internationally experienced facilities.

Pre-operative protocols differ by clinic and specialty. **Verify medication hold instructions directly with the clinic** after sharing your list; do not rely solely on generic guides or forum anecdotes.

## Red Flags in Medication Screening

* Clinics that proceed without collecting written medication history
* Coordinators who tell you to hide supplement use to avoid delays
* No anesthesia review before sedation-based procedures
* Refusal to contact your home physician for high-risk holds

## Protect Your Safety with Transparency

Your complete medication story belongs on the clinic chart, not in omitted details. Prepare documents early, ask coordinated stop plans, and treat supplements with the same seriousness as prescriptions.
