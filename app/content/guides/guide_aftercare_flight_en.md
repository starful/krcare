Booking a flight home before your body is ready is one of the most common mistakes international patients make after surgery in Korea. Cabin pressure, long sitting periods, and limited access to emergency care mid-flight all interact with your healing in ways that are easy to underestimate when you're eager to get home.

## Why Flight Timing Isn't Just About "Days After Surgery"

Many patients assume there's a universal rule — like "wait 7 days" — but recovery is procedure-specific. A minor dermatology treatment and a multi-hour surgical procedure carry very different risks in the air. Two factors matter most:

- **Cabin pressure changes.** Even modern pressurized cabins sit at an effective altitude of 6,000–8,000 feet. This can worsen swelling, affect healing tissue, and in rare cases increase clotting risk after certain procedures.
- **Immobility during long flights.** Sitting for hours without moving raises the risk of deep vein thrombosis (DVT), which is a real concern after many surgeries, not just cosmetic ones.

Your surgeon — not a general rule of thumb — should be the one who tells you when it's safe to fly, based on your specific procedure and how your recovery is progressing.

## Building Your Aftercare Timeline

### 1. Book Flexible Travel From the Start
When you first book your trip, choose flights and accommodation with **flexible change policies**. Locking in a rigid return date before you've even had your consultation puts pressure on your recovery decisions later. If your clinic later advises extending your stay, you want that to be a simple date change, not a financial loss.

### 2. Confirm the Expected Recovery Stay During Consultation
Ask your clinic — ideally during a virtual consultation before you travel — for a realistic range of days you'll need to stay in Korea after your procedure. Build in a buffer beyond their minimum estimate, since healing doesn't always follow the average timeline.

### 3. Track Stitches and Wound Closure
If your procedure involves **stitches**, ask specifically:
- When are stitches expected to be removed, or do they dissolve on their own?
- Does the wound need to be fully closed and dry before flying, or is partial healing acceptable?
- Will you need a follow-up visit right before departure to confirm the wound is stable for travel?

Flying with an incompletely closed wound raises infection risk in a recycled-air cabin environment, so don't treat this step as optional.

### 4. Monitor Swelling Before You Fly
**Swelling** is often the most visible sign your body gives you about readiness to travel. Ask your clinic:
- What level of swelling is considered "normal" at the point you're planning to fly?
- Are there compression garments, medications, or positioning techniques (like keeping the area elevated) recommended for the flight itself?
- Should you request a wheelchair or extra boarding time to avoid prolonged standing before boarding?

### 5. Get Written Fitness-to-Fly Documentation
Ask your clinic for a **written note or medical summary** confirming you are cleared to fly, especially if you're wearing bandages, compression garments, or carrying post-op medication. This can help with airport security, airline crew questions, and immigration on the other end.

## Language, Interpreters, and Communication

Aftercare instructions are easy to misunderstand if there's a language barrier, and mistakes here directly affect your flight timeline:

- Ask upfront whether the clinic has an **in-house interpreter** or works with international patient coordinators.
- Request that critical instructions — medication schedules, wound care, and flight clearance — be provided in **writing**, in your language, not just spoken during a rushed appointment.
- If a discharge summary or clearance letter is only provided in Korean, ask for an English (or your language) translation before you leave.

## Deposits and Financial Planning Around Recovery Extensions

Because recovery timelines can shift, plan your finances accordingly:

- Clarify what portion of your deposit is refundable if your recovery takes longer than expected and you need to extend hotel or clinic follow-up visits.
- Ask whether extended follow-up appointments (beyond the originally quoted package) carry additional fees.
- Avoid wiring large deposits to personal bank accounts rather than the clinic's official business account — this is a common scam pattern targeting international patients.

## Questions to Ask Your Clinic Before You Book Your Return Flight

Bring this list to your pre-departure follow-up appointment:

1. **"Based on my healing today, am I cleared to fly on my current departure date?"**
2. **"Are my stitches fully removed or dissolved, and is the incision site sealed?"**
3. **"What swelling level is safe for cabin pressure changes, and should I wear compression garments during the flight?"**
4. **"Should I avoid alcohol, caffeine, or specific medications before or during the flight?"**
5. **"What symptoms during the flight would mean I need to seek emergency care on landing?"**
6. **"Can I get a written fitness-to-fly summary I can show airline staff or my doctor at home?"**

## Red Flags to Watch For

- **A clinic that clears you to fly without a hands-on final check** of your wound and swelling in person.
- **Pressure to stick to your original flight date** even when you're reporting pain, unusual swelling, or discharge from the incision site.
- **No written discharge or aftercare summary** provided in a language you understand.
- **Vague answers about DVT risk or compression garment recommendations** for longer flights (generally flights over 4 hours warrant extra caution after many procedures).
- **Refusal to provide contact information** for post-departure questions once you've left Korea.

If you notice any of these, it's worth getting a second opinion or simply asking more direct questions before finalizing your return date.
